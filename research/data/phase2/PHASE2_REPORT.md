Preview score = share × mean severity × addressability. It's indicative only; Phase 3 implements the real scoring function.

**Autopay mandate setup & execution failures** — Everything around the autopay mandate: a ₹500–2,000 SIP shows a ₹15,000 UPI Autopay limit, a ₹1 lakh e-mandate OTP, an end date decades away or a ₹5,000 initial investment, and investors fear that amount will be debited (the mandate is a ceiling, not a charge). Autopay can't be set up, SIPs get cancelled repeatedly, autopay fails after the first payment and the investor is debited twice, and nobody knows whether turning off autopay in a UPI app stops the SIP. Part of this is Groww's screens; part sits with banks, NPCI and UPI apps.

**Influencer-led start on thin understanding** — People start their first SIP because a creator's video motivated them, often saying they knew nothing before. Their first questions show how little the start rested on: is the next installment automatic, is there a penalty to cancel, can the amount change, how many installments before stopping.

**Basic concept gaps** — Questions about what NAV is and which NAV applies, how compounding works in growth funds, direct vs regular plans, ELSS lock-in, daily SIPs, and how different SIP types differ.

**Debit & allotment visibility gap** — Money leaves the bank but the app shows 'in progress', nothing, or a different amount; investors can't tell where their money is, when the next installment will be debited, or how it reaches the fund.

**Seeking fund picks & return assurances** — Beginners ask which fund or company is best, whether a 12% return is assured, or what someone would personally choose in their place. Groww can't answer these without breaching SEBI's no-advice rules.

**Fear of loss & early negative returns** — New investors ask whether SIP money can 'doob' (vanish), whether it is insured like a bank deposit, and panic when an early SIP shows a loss — often after starting on someone else's recommendation. When the loss arrives together with bank bounce charges, they want out.

**Debit date vs cash-flow mismatch** — The SIP date doesn't match when money actually arrives: salary lands on the 7th while the SIP debits on the 5th, the balance dips just before payday, or income is irregular (freelancers, students). The debit bounces, the bank charges a penalty, and the investor pauses or stops. Surfaced by the primary survey and interviews.

**Rigid SIP controls (pause / skip / cancel / modify)** — Investors want to know how to stop, pause, cancel before the first installment, change the amount or date, and what happens if money isn't there on the SIP date — and whether any of it costs them.

## 2.3 Discovery answers

**Q1. Why do first-time investors start a SIP, and what are their expectations?**

Someone else starts them. In the survey of 27 first-time investors, 14 started because a friend, colleague, office talk or family member nudged them and 9 because of a YouTube or Instagram creator; only 4 did their own research. The public corpus shows the same pull of creators. Expectations are that a SIP is hassle-free and automatic, and often that it is safe or that a specific return will come (“will I get 12%?”). The first time the payment plumbing shows through, that expectation breaks.

Evidence: `SV-S14`, `YT-58a062d811`, `YT-686d890c18`

**Q2. How does behavior/emotion evolve from Month 1 through Month 8–12?**

Before starting: curious and cautious. At setup: alarm at the mandate ceiling (19 of 27 survey respondents were thrown by a limit far above their SIP). Months 2–4 are the danger window: 22 of the 32 survey and interview records place their decisive moment there, nearly always the first failed debit, followed by a bank penalty, an unexplained status, or no way to retry. Emotion moves from worry to embarrassment or panic, then to inaction: the SIP is paused, left “to sort out later”, or silently lapses. People who get past month 4 without a failure keep running. The data still has no records from months 9–12.

Evidence: `YT-ac0a7a0881`, `IN-R01`, `IN-R04`

**Q3. What triggers a decision to pause, skip, or cancel?**

The first failed instalment. 20 of 27 survey respondents had an instalment fail, get skipped or get paused, and only 2 of those 20 are still active; all 7 who never had one are still running. The failure itself usually comes from the SIP date landing before salary or a low balance, a bank-side or UPI glitch, or a reason the investor never learns (5 of 27). What turns it into a stop is the bank's bounce charge (₹295–450, often felt more than the instalment), no in-app explanation, and no way to pay that month. Early flat or negative returns trigger pauses too, and money stuck “in progress” triggers cancellations.

Evidence: `IN-R05`, `SV-S10`, `IN-R03`

**Q4. What do users monitor in the app, and how often?**

They reconcile bank SMS against the app around debit dates: was money taken, and did units appear? 5 of 27 survey respondents saw units more than 3 days after the debit, and in interviews that gap was read as money lost. Outside debit days most don't watch at all, which is why a failed instalment can go unnoticed for two months. Continuers describe a light monthly check of the portfolio.

Evidence: `IN-R02`, `IN-R04`, `YT-a39c7d51fd`

**Q5. What inside-app elements create confusion or misplaced blame?**

The autopay screen showing ₹15,000 or ₹25,000 for a ₹500–2,500 SIP (70% of survey respondents); status labels like “Mandate Expired”, “Payment Initiated” or “In Progress” that then fail silently; a warning icon with no action; and no button to retry or pay the missed month. Bank bounce charges are blamed on the investment app, and support calling them “not our problem” deepens the distrust.

Evidence: `IN-R07`, `IN-R08`, `YT-c55a697c2d`

**Q6. What outside-app workarounds do investors use?**

The main escape hatch is revoking the autopay mandate directly in a UPI app (PhonePe, Google Pay or BHIM) to stop further bounce charges, which ends the SIP without the investment app seeing a cancellation. Others search Google, YouTube or Reddit, ask a friend or roommate (one switched to a net-banking mandate that way), or ask creators in YouTube comments. The most common response is no response: 13 of 27 survey respondents did nothing, and none of those 13 are still active.

Evidence: `IN-R06`, `SV-S10`, `SV-S18`

**Q7. What distinguishes continuers from drop-offs?**

Continuers had someone explain the mechanics up front (a parent, sibling or senior) and picked a SIP date a few days after salary, so the first debits went through. Every survey respondent who never had a failed instalment is still active, and 2 of 3 on a bank e-mandate are active versus 7 of 23 on UPI Autopay. Drop-offs hit one failure early and had no clear way to recover. The public corpus adds that people anchored to a concrete goal talk about continuing.

Evidence: `IN-R09`, `SV-S17`, `IN-R01`

**Q8. Are there different churn patterns across investor segments?**

Income type matters most: neither freelancer in the survey is still active and both students struggled, because fixed-date automatic debits don't fit irregular income. Among salaried first-jobbers 4 of 13 are active versus 4 of 10 with 2+ years' experience. Creator-led starters fare worst (2 of 9 active) next to friend- or colleague-led ones (3 of 10). In the public corpus, Hinglish speakers are over-represented in mandate and debit confusion. The survey is small, so treat these as directions to test, not rates.

Evidence: `SV-S24`, `SV-S28`, `YT-ac0a7a0881`

**Q9. What consistent unmet needs surface across the corpus?**

(1) A plain note at setup that the mandate amount is a ceiling, not a charge. (2) A SIP date chosen around salary day, plus a balance reminder the day before the debit. (3) A same-day “retry / pay this month now” after a failed debit. (4) A proactive alert when an instalment fails, with the reason in plain language instead of jargon. (5) Reassurance on who charges the bounce fee and that a SIP bounce doesn't touch the credit score. (6) A clear unit-allotment timeline. (7) Easy pause and resume. (8) SEBI-safe education for “which fund / how much return” questions.

Evidence: `IN-R01`, `IN-R10`, `YT-a39c7d51fd`

## 2.6 PM correction cases

**CC-1** (`YT-c55a697c2d`, rule `RULE_UPI_MANDATE_LIMIT`)

- Naive reading: Billing error: app tries to charge ₹15,000/month on a ₹500 SIP. Theme: overcharging. Severity 5.
- Why it's wrong: ₹15,000 is the UPI Autopay mandate's maximum limit, not the debit. Only the SIP amount is debited each month. Nothing was charged; the investor is confused, not harmed.
- Correct: Theme: Autopay mandate setup & execution failures. Severity 3 (worry, no money lost).

**CC-2** (`YT-0a654eb8b6`, rule `RULE_UPI_REVOKE`)

- Naive reading: How-to question about using Google Pay. Theme: concept gap. Severity 2, low priority.
- Why it's wrong: UPI Autopay mandates can be paused or revoked from any UPI app. Turning it off in Google Pay silently stops the Groww SIP's debits without the investor cancelling anything in Groww — a churn path outside Groww's control that the investor doesn't understand.
- Correct: Theme: Autopay mandate setup & execution failures (platform-dependent). Severity 3.

**CC-3** (`YT-686d890c18`, rule `SEBI_NO_RETURN_PRED`)

- Naive reading: Concept question about Nifty 50 returns. Answer: 'yes, ~12% long-term is typical'. Addressability 1.0.
- Why it's wrong: Confirming or predicting a return is prohibited under SEBI rules for a non-RIA platform. The need is real but can only be met with education, so addressability must be penalised.
- Correct: Theme: Seeking fund picks & return assurances. SEBI_NO_RETURN_PRED → addressability 0.2.

**CC-4** (`YT-781475ace9`, rule `RULE_NACH_BOUNCE`)

- Naive reading: Billing complaint: Groww is charging extra fees and the SIP is stuck. Theme: mandate failure. Severity 3.
- Why it's wrong: The extra deductions are NACH/UPI mandate bounce charges levied by the investor's bank when the balance is short, not Groww fees, so 'billing complaint' blames the wrong party. What drives the message is the loss the investor opens with: the charges compound a loss they already feel, and they want out.
- Correct: Theme: Fear of loss & early negative returns. Severity 5 (wants to stop, can't).

**CC-5** (`PS-4bfd911de1`, rule `RULE_NAV_ALLOC_DELAY`)

- Naive reading: App performance complaint about slow status. Theme: app bug. Severity 3.
- Why it's wrong: Mutual-fund orders move through payment, order placement and unit allotment over a few business days; 'in progress' is normal. The reviewer also reports a real drop-off — their brother never invested again after the same experience — which makes this a retention signal, not a cosmetic bug.
- Correct: Theme: Debit & allotment visibility gap. Severity 5 (documented abandonment).

**CC-6** (`IN-R10`, rule `RULE_SIP_BOUNCE_NO_CIBIL`)

- Naive reading: Investor panicking about the investment losing value. Theme: fear of loss. Severity 3.
- Why it's wrong: The panic is about the credit score, not the market: a bank SMS about a failed mandate made the investor think a SIP bounce would hurt their CIBIL score, as a missed loan EMI would. A SIP is not a loan, so a failed SIP debit is not reported to credit bureaus; only the bank's return charge applies. With no reassurance in the app, the investor cancelled everything.
- Correct: Theme: Autopay mandate setup & execution failures. Severity 5 (cancelled).
